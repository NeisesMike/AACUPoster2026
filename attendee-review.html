AAC&U CAIHE Attendee Review

A critical read-through from the perspective of a conference attendee who arrived via the poster QR code and wants to understand the teaching approach, evaluate the evidence, and identify ideas to adapt.

Audience questions

Questions an attendee may ask

Core idea and framework

  • What are the specific “Critical Teaching Behaviors,” and how are they demonstrated in each course example?
  • What central claim connects the three cases? The synthesis and conclusions are still placeholders.
  • How does “Teaching First, AI Second” translate into course-design decisions, beyond being a memorable title?

Course context and design

  • What course, student population, institution, term, and class size does each case describe?
  • What is the intended purpose of the Economics course?
  • In the Math case, how were scenarios aligned to academic programs, and what does weighting by program representation mean?
  • Which AI tools and student-use guidance were involved, and what did students do without AI?
  • What does “Align/Asses” mean in the business-writing draft heading—is “Assess” intended? What were the student roles in the AI-use scale?
  • Which activities, instructions, or assessment designs could another instructor adapt?

Evidence and interpretation

  • How many students contributed to each result, and what instruments, prompts, or analysis methods were used?
  • For Math, what do the reflection results, word cloud, and before/after GIFT data show? What do the GIFT measures capture, and over what period?
  • For Economics, which assessments support the reported growth, and how was growth determined? The site avoids claiming AI caused it, but needs evidence and methods.
  • For business writing, what is the denominator behind “two-thirds,” and how was “deep operational” use assigned or validated?
  • Is the claim that oral presentations may lower cheating pressure a hypothesis, a cited research finding, or a result from this course?

Sources and follow-up

  • Where can attendees find the Critical Teaching Behaviors framework and research validating it?
  • What are the complete citations and working links for the AAC&U VALUE Rubric, The Opposite of Cheating, and the student AI-use scale?
  • Where are the TILT example and write-ups of the AI-use-scale levels?
  • Does the contact link open a message to the presenter for questions or requests for materials?

Author follow-up

Content gaps to resolve before public QR use

  • Replace visible `[Add ... here!]` placeholders with supplied content or remove items that will not be included.
  • Add cross-case synthesis and conclusions; do not imply a shared outcome until supported by the presenter’s analysis.
  • Add the Math reflection context, word cloud, GIFT data, and interpretation.
  • Add the Economics course purpose and the measures, student count, and evidence supporting its reported findings.
  • Add the writing-course student count, scale categories and roles, details on “deep operational” classification, and a shareable TILT assignment if available.
  • Complete and verify citations and links for the Critical Teaching Behaviors framework and research base, student AI-use scale (draft attribution: “Perkins, Ferze 2024”), AAC&U VALUE Rubric, and The Opposite of Cheating.
  • Confirm whether “Align/Asses” is intentional or should read “Align/Assess.”
  • Add course context such as term, level, enrollment, and setting where appropriate and shareable.

Visitor experience

Presentation and usability critiques

  • Keep the first screen focused on the title, a brief explanation, and obvious paths to the cases; QR visitors may have little time or context.
  • Keep the three cases under consistent purpose, design/AI use, and evidence headings, while exposing each case’s methods and limitations.
  • The explicit placeholders avoid invented claims, but make the site read as a draft. Resolve or deliberately hide them before sharing the QR code publicly.
  • Resource names without verified destinations are not yet useful to attendees; add canonical links and complete citations before publication.
  • Confirm the contact link opens the correct email client and address. A practical “what you can take away” element still needs author-supplied content.
  • Test on a narrow phone screen and with keyboard navigation. Check the horizontally scrollable navigation, sticky-header anchor positioning, contrast, and long course-outcome passages.

Recommended order

Highest-priority attendee improvements

  1. Complete the shared takeaway and conclusions.
  2. Supply enough method and sample context for attendees to interpret each finding.
  3. Add verified framework and citation links plus shareable assignment examples.
  4. Replace or remove all draft placeholders before distributing the poster QR code.
  5. Confirm the presenter contact link and add concise transfer-to-practice guidance.
