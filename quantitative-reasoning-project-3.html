Exponential Growth & Decay

Choose a scenario

Project Scenarios

Choose one of the following scenarios:

Scenario A

According to legend, placing rice on a chessboard square that doubles each time leads to an astronomical total: by the 54th square the cumulative rice exceeds annual world production – a famous illustration of exponential growth.

Scenario B

Radiocarbon dating relies on exponential decay: carbon-14 (half-life ~5,730 years) decays so that only half a sample’s C-14 remains after ~5.73 millennia.

Scenario C

In early 2020, COVID-19 outbreaks grew exponentially – for example, cases in many regions doubled roughly every 3 days – until public health measures slowed the spread.

All scenarios are designed to address the same underlying learning goals. Your work will be graded using the same rubric, regardless of which scenario you choose.

Exponential growth and decay

Topic-Specific Guidance

For each scenario, include a graph, table, bar chart, or other visualization to help explain what's happening.

Scenario A

Is it true that by the 54th square the cumulative rice exceeds annual world production? How much rice would this reward cost in total? Choose visualizations that make the “doubling” process clear.

Scenario B

Give examples of things so old they had to be radiocarbon dated. This isn't chemistry class, but try to explain why/how radioactive decay is an exponential process.

Scenario C

Consider what might have happened had no public health measures been introduced. Assume that the rate of new cases continued doubling every three days—in that case, how many days would it take for the whole world to be infected?
