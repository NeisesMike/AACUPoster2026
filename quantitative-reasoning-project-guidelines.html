Quantitative reasoning project

Project Guidelines

Purpose

Purpose

This project asks you to use quantitative reasoning to analyze a real situation related to this week's topic.

You will make a clear quantitative claim, support it with evidence, and explain your reasoning in a way that could be shared with others.

The goal is not to do as much math as possible, but to use appropriate mathematics thoughtfully and clearly.

Your assignment

Your Task

Prepare a written or recorded report that analyzes your chosen scenario using quantitative reasoning appropriate to this week's topic.

Your report should:

  • Make at least one clear quantitative claim.
  • Support that claim with numerical, algebraic, graphical, or statistical evidence.
  • Explain your reasoning in words, not just calculations.

The report should be suitable for presentation to the class, even if you are not selected to present.

Choose a format

Deliverable Options

Choose one format:

  • Written report (PDF or document)
  • Recorded presentation (video or narrated slides)

There is no required length. Quality, clarity, and reasoning matter far more than word count or runtime.

Build your report

What Your Report Must Include

  1. Quantitative Claim

    State a clear claim that can be evaluated using data or mathematics.

    Example: “Based on the model, Option A becomes more cost-effective after approximately 14 months.”

  2. Evidence

    Provide quantitative evidence that supports your claim. This may include:

    • Calculations
    • Tables or graphs
    • Algebraic models
    • Data summaries

    All quantities should be clearly labeled with units where appropriate.

  3. Reasoning

    Explain how your evidence supports your claim.

    • Why does this mathematics apply here?
    • How should your results be interpreted?
    • What assumptions are you making?

    This explanation is often the most important part of the project.

  4. Assumptions & Limitations

    Identify assumptions you made and discuss how they affect your conclusions.

    • What did you simplify?
    • What information was missing?
    • How might the conclusions change if assumptions change?

VALUE Rubric overview

Quantitative Reasoning Expectations

Your work will be evaluated using the following dimensions of quantitative reasoning:

  • Interpretation: You accurately interpret quantitative information and results in context.
  • Representation: You use tables, graphs, equations, or numerical summaries effectively and correctly.
  • Calculation: You perform mathematical procedures accurately and appropriately.
  • Application / Analysis: You choose mathematical tools that make sense for the situation and apply them meaningfully.
  • Assumptions: You clearly identify assumptions and recognize their impact on conclusions.
  • Communication: Your writing or presentation is clear, well-organized, and understandable to others.

Important: A strong project does not need to excel equally in every dimension. Thoughtful reasoning and clear explanation can earn a strong score even if some mathematics is simple.

Sources and collaboration

Academic Integrity & Sources

  • Cite any data sources you use.
  • Use your own reasoning and explanations.
  • Collaboration is allowed when brainstorming and learning, but projects must be prepared and written individually, in general. If you would like to work as a pair, please let me know and I'm sure that will be okay. Pair projects will be held to a higher standard than individual projects.

Sharing your work

Presentation Note

A small number of students will be invited to present their work in class each week. All students must submit a report that is presentation-ready, even if they are not selected.

Keep in mind

Reminder

Each project emphasizes reasoning, interpretation, and communication. Mathematics is a tool — not the final product.
